# Open Research Questions — Quantum Energy and Scale Limits

**Owner:** Cosmos Explorer / Gravity Research  
**Recorded:** 2026-10-26  
**Status:** Open investigation, not a new theory or an experimental discovery.

This is an ongoing addendum to the [eleven original research questions](Gravity-Research/Gravity-Research.md). The original migrated documents and historical findings remain unchanged. [Working gravity hypothesis](WORKING_HYPOTHESIS.md) remains the central hypothesis, not an established physical law.

## O-12 — Energy accounting, quantum entanglement, and conservation laws

**Question:** What quantitative, independently reproducible observation would constitute a genuine energy-accounting discrepancy, rather than a missing reservoir, faulty system boundary, measurement error, or misapplication of a model's domain?

**Related questions:**
- Does creating or measuring entanglement change the total energy of the entire experimental system after accounting for preparation, measurement, heat, and work?
- What observable distinguishes nonlocal measurement correlations from an actual transfer of usable energy?
- Under what conditions can entanglement-assisted protocols enable local energy extraction, and what classical communication and work inputs are required?
- Could a measured change in the stress-energy distribution create a measurable gravitational effect, and how would that prediction be separated from instrumental artifacts?
- When does a conserved **global** energy exist? How do local energy-momentum conservation in general relativity and global energy accounting differ in expanding cosmological spacetimes?

**Established evidence / limitations:** Quantum entanglement and Bell-test violations are demonstrated; neither implies controllable faster-than-light transfer of messages or usable energy. Quantum energy teleportation protocols have experimental realizations, but require local operations and classical communication and do not demonstrate energy creation. General relativity uses local stress-energy conservation; globally conserved energy need not be defined for an arbitrary time-dependent curved spacetime.

**Proposed discriminating test:** Define experimental boundaries and clock/frame conventions; record initial/final energy and every known preparation, measurement, heat, work, and communication flow with uncertainties. Compare entangled and matched non-entangled controls. Specify a prediction and statistical threshold **before** taking new measurements; reproduce independently. A correlation alone fails the evidence threshold for energy transfer. An observed mismatch requires first attempting to account for omitted reservoirs and systematics.

**Outcome:** Unanswered; no experiment has been run by Cosmos Explorer to investigate O-12.

## O-13 — Domains of Newtonian laws and competing gravitational predictions

**Question:** Which precise observations distinguish a true failure of Newtonian approximations from an unmodeled source of gravity, a measurement error, or a calculation outside its stated domain?

**Terminology:** A scientific law expresses a reliable relationship within a demonstrated scope; a theory is an explanatory framework. Neither is guaranteed universal truth. Newtonian mechanics remains highly accurate in its ordinary weak-field, low-speed domain, while measured relativistic and quantum phenomena require other descriptions. A galaxy rotation discrepancy alone does not tell us which source of missing acceleration or model failure applies.

**Proposed test:** Identify velocity, gravity regime, spatial scale, matter budget, precision and uncertainties before comparing Newtonian, relativistic, and candidate alternatives. Reproduce local ephemeris controls, test held-out galaxy rotations with measured mass distributions, and cross-check lensing, clusters, and cosmological constraints. Do not treat a successful fit as proof of a unique explanation.

**Outcome:** Open as a research question. Demonstrated limits of Newtonian approximations should not be mislabeled a novel project discovery.

## O-14 — Mass-energy, the Higgs mechanism, and gravitational response

**Question:** When predicting gravity from a physical system, are the independently measurable contributions from rest mass, binding energy, field energy, pressure, and motion modeled with justified uncertainties, and can any omitted contribution explain a measurable residual?

**Observed foundations:** The Higgs **field** contributes to the rest masses of elementary quarks, charged leptons, and W/Z bosons; the Higgs **boson** is its observed excitation and is not a mechanism that creates gravity. Much of ordinary proton/neutron mass arises from quantum chromodynamics (strong-interaction energy). In its center-of-momentum frame, a system's total rest energy and invariant mass obey $E_0=Mc^2$. Transferring energy into a system from outside can increase its invariant mass; redistributing energy internally does not by itself increase the total mass of a closed system. Einstein gravity couples to stress-energy, not only to rest masses from the Higgs field. Inertial mass and gravitational response are experimentally equivalent to high precision, but this does not itself supply a complete quantum theory of gravity.

**Test:** Take a system with independently measured energy changes, specify its full energy-momentum and uncertainties, and estimate the gravitational signal. Compare predictions to measurements where sensitivity permits; do not assign extra energy simply because constituents are entangled. Distinguish internal binding energy already included in a measured composite mass from any additional proposed component to avoid double-counting.

**Outcome:** Open inventory and precision question; it does not establish an anomalous gravitational effect.

## Research evidence policy

- Label each entry **observed**, **derived**, **model-dependent inference**, or **hypothesis / untested question**.
- Preserve exact datasets, instrument and observation epochs, source versions, units, measurement uncertainty, analysis code, and competing predictions.
- Test conservation laws within the conditions where they are defined. A challenge is allowed, but must be supported by an independently reproducible quantitative discrepancy.
- Record negative results, alternative explanations, and unresolved questions without adding unsupported physical mechanisms to explain galaxy rotation.
